\begin{abstract}
DNA data storage has emerged as a promising archival medium offering extreme density and durability. However, existing approaches often produce synthetic DNA sequences that violate biological synthesis constraints, lack integrated tamper detection, and provide no standardized evaluation framework. This paper presents BioVerse, an end-to-end DNA data encoding system that introduces three contributions: (1) a biologically-constrained encoding scheme using a rotation cipher that enforces homopolymer run limits, GC-content balance, and restriction enzyme site avoidance while maintaining lossless reversibility; (2) a dual-purpose AI watermarking system where LSTM-generated DNA fingerprints simultaneously provide biological camouflage and embed SHA-256 integrity hashes for tamper detection; and (3) a quantitative evaluation framework that benchmarks encoding density, security, error resilience, biological realism, and pipeline performance. Experimental results demonstrate that constrained encoding achieves full biological constraint compliance with measurable realism improvement over naive 2-bit mapping, while maintaining lossless data recovery through AES-128 encryption and Reed-Solomon error correction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The exponential growth of digital data has intensified the search for storage media beyond conventional magnetic and optical technologies. DNA, nature's information storage molecule, offers a theoretical maximum density of 2 bits per nucleotide and an estimated half-life of over 500 years under appropriate conditions \cite{b1}. Recent works by Church et al.\ \cite{b2}, Goldman et al.\ \cite{b3}, and Erlich and Zielinski \cite{b4} have demonstrated the feasibility of encoding arbitrary digital data into synthetic DNA sequences.

However, several challenges persist in practical DNA data storage systems:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Synthesis constraints}: DNA synthesis technologies (e.g., phosphoramidite chemistry, enzymatic synthesis) impose constraints on sequence composition. Long homopolymer runs ($\geq 4$ consecutive identical bases) reduce synthesis yield, extreme GC content ($<$30\% or $>$70\%) causes secondary structures, and restriction enzyme recognition sites can cause unintended cleavage during handling \cite{b5}.

\item \textbf{Tamper detection}: Existing systems store integrity information externally (separate checksums or manifests), creating a vulnerability where the DNA payload could be modified independently of its integrity metadata.

\item \textbf{Standardized evaluation}: There is no unified framework for comparing DNA encoding systems across security, error resilience, biological realism, and storage efficiency dimensions simultaneously.
\end{enumerate}

This paper presents \textbf{BioVerse}, a system that addresses these challenges through biologically-constrained encoding, integrated integrity watermarking, and a formal evaluation framework. The key contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item A \textit{constraint-aware rotation cipher} that enforces homopolymer limits ($\leq 3$), GC-content balance (30--70\% per 20-base window), and restriction enzyme site avoidance while maintaining lossless reversibility through sideband rotation metadata.
\item A \textit{dual-purpose AI watermark} where LSTM-generated DNA fingerprints simultaneously provide biological camouflage and embed SHA-256 integrity hashes for tamper detection without external checksums.
\item A \textit{quantitative evaluation framework} producing IEEE-ready metrics across five dimensions: security, error resilience, biological realism, storage efficiency, and pipeline performance.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section~II reviews related work, Section~III describes the system architecture, Section~IV presents the evaluation framework, Section~V details the implementation, Section~VI reports experimental results, Section~VII discusses limitations, and Section~VIII concludes with future directions.

Church et al.\ \cite{b2} demonstrated the first large-scale DNA data storage using a single-bit-per-base mapping but did not address homopolymer runs or other synthesis constraints. Goldman et al.\ \cite{b3} introduced a rotating ternary code to avoid homopolymers, achieving 1.58 bits/nt, but did not address GC balance or restriction sites. Grass et al.\ \cite{b6} applied Reed-Solomon codes for error correction on silica-encapsulated DNA but imposed no biological constraints on the encoded sequences.

Erlich and Zielinski \cite{b4} achieved near-theoretical-maximum density (1.98 bits/nt) using DNA Fountain codes with Luby Transform encoding. Their approach screens generated oligos for biological viability and discards those that fail, which is effective but wasteful. Organick et al.\ \cite{b7} demonstrated random access in large-scale DNA storage but did not address constraint enforcement.

Yazdi et al.\ \cite{b5} proposed rewritable DNA storage with constrained coding, providing theoretical foundations for biological constraint enforcement. However, their work focused on constrained coding theory rather than a complete, deployable system.

BioVerse differs from prior work in three respects: (1) it enforces \textit{multiple simultaneous constraints} (homopolymer, GC, and restriction sites) through a deterministic rotation cipher rather than screening or rejection; (2) it embeds integrity verification \textit{within the DNA sequence itself} rather than relying on external metadata; and (3) it provides a standardized evaluation framework for cross-system comparison.

\section{System Architecture}

The BioVerse pipeline consists of six stages operating in sequence:

\begin{equation}
\text{File} \xrightarrow{\text{AES}} \text{CT} \xrightarrow{\text{RS}} \text{ECC} \xrightarrow{\text{Bin}} \text{DNA}_c \xrightarrow{\text{FP}} \text{DNA File}
\label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}

\noindent where CT denotes ciphertext, RS denotes Reed-Solomon encoding, $\text{DNA}_c$ denotes constrained DNA, and FP denotes fingerprint attachment.

\subsection{Encryption Layer}

Input file bytes are encrypted using AES-128 in Cipher Block Chaining (CBC) mode with PKCS7 padding. A random 16-byte initialization vector (IV) is generated for each encryption operation and prepended to the ciphertext. The key space is $2^{128} \approx 3.4 \times 10^{38}$, yielding an estimated brute-force time of approximately $5.39 \times 10^{21}$ years at $10^9$ keys/second.

\subsection{Error Correction Layer}

The ciphertext is encoded using a Reed-Solomon code with 32 parity symbols. This configuration corrects up to 16 symbol errors and detects up to 32 errors per 255-byte block, providing resilience against base mutations, insertions, and deletions that may occur during DNA synthesis, storage, or sequencing.

\subsection{Biologically-Constrained DNA Encoding}

The binary representation of the ECC-protected ciphertext is converted to DNA using a \textit{constraint-aware rotation cipher}. The encoding proceeds as follows:

\subsubsection{Naive Mapping}
Each 2-bit pair is initially mapped using the standard encoding: $00 \to A$, $01 \to T$, $10 \to G$, $11 \to C$.

\subsubsection{Constraint Verification}
Before appending the candidate base to the growing sequence, three biological constraints are evaluated:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Homopolymer limit}: The candidate base must not create a run of more than $k = 3$ consecutive identical bases.
\item \textbf{GC-content balance}: A sliding window of $w = 20$ bases must maintain GC content within the range $[0.30, 0.70]$.
\item \textbf{Restriction site avoidance}: The tail of the sequence plus the candidate must not form recognition sites for five common restriction enzymes: EcoRI (\texttt{GAATTC}), BamHI (\texttt{GGATCC}), HindIII (\texttt{AAGCTT}), XhoI (\texttt{CTCGAG}), or EcoRV (\texttt{GATATC}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Rotation Mechanism}
If the naive base violates any constraint, it is rotated cyclically through the base alphabet ($A \to T \to G \to C \to A$) up to 3 times until a valid base is found. For position $i$, let $b_i^{(0)}$ denote the naive base and $r_i \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ denote the rotation count. The encoded base is:

\begin{equation}
b_i = \text{BASES}\left[\left(\text{idx}(b_i^{(0)}) + r_i\right) \bmod 4\right]
\label{eq:rotation}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\text{idx}(A)=0$, $\text{idx}(T)=1$, $\text{idx}(G)=2$, $\text{idx}(C)=3$.

\subsubsection{Sideband Metadata}
The rotation flags $r_i$ are packed as 2-bit values (4 per byte) and stored as hex-encoded metadata in the DNA file header. The storage overhead for the rotation sideband is:

\begin{equation}
O_{\text{rot}} = \frac{N}{4} \text{ bytes for } N \text{ encoded bases}
\label{eq:overhead}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Decoding}
Given the constrained DNA sequence and the rotation flags, the original binary is recovered by reversing the rotation:

\begin{equation}
b_i^{(0)} = \text{BASES}\left[\left(\text{idx}(b_i) - r_i\right) \bmod 4\right]
\label{eq:decode_rotation}
\end{equation}

\noindent followed by the standard reverse mapping ($A \to 00$, $T \to 01$, $G \to 10$, $C \to 11$).

\subsection{AI DNA Fingerprint Generation}

A biologically realistic DNA watermark of 120 bases is generated using one of two methods:

\begin{itemize}
\item An \textbf{LSTM neural network} trained on real genomic sequences from \textit{Homo sapiens} (chr1), \textit{Escherichia coli}, and \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}, using character-level sequence modeling with temperature-controlled sampling.
\item A \textbf{first-order Markov chain} with dinucleotide transition probabilities derived from genomic statistics, used as a fallback when TensorFlow is unavailable.
\end{itemize}

The fingerprint serves as a biological camouflage layer. AES-encrypted data tends toward uniform base distribution because the ciphertext has high entropy. When concatenated with the AI-generated fingerprint (which has non-uniform, biologically realistic statistics), the overall sequence more closely resembles natural genomic DNA.

\subsection{Integrity Hash Embedding}

A SHA-256 hash of the payload DNA is computed, and the first 32 hexadecimal characters (128 bits of integrity) are encoded as DNA bases using a 4-to-1 hex-to-base mapping:

\begin{equation}
\{0\text{--}3\} \to A, \quad \{4\text{--}7\} \to T, \quad \{8\text{--}b\} \to G, \quad \{c\text{--}f\} \to C
\label{eq:hex_mapping}
\end{equation}

The 32 resulting bases are inserted into the AI fingerprint between marker sequences (\texttt{AGTC}), producing a dual-purpose watermark. During decoding, the embedded hash bases are extracted and compared against a freshly computed hash of the decoded payload. A mismatch indicates tampering:

\begin{equation}
\text{Tampered} =
\begin{cases}
\text{True} & \text{if } H_{\text{embedded}} \neq H_{\text{computed}} \\
\text{False} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:tamper}
\end{equation}

\section{Evaluation Framework}

BioVerse includes a formal evaluation module that produces standardized metrics across five dimensions. This framework enables reproducible comparison between encoding strategies.

\subsection{Storage Efficiency}

The effective encoding density is measured as:

\begin{equation}
\eta = \frac{B_{\text{original}} \times 8}{N_{\text{DNA}}} \quad \text{(bits per base)}
\label{eq:density}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $B_{\text{original}}$ is the original file size in bytes and $N_{\text{DNA}}$ is the number of DNA bases in the payload. The encoding efficiency relative to the theoretical maximum (2 bits/base) is:

\begin{equation}
E = \frac{\eta}{2.0} \times 100\%
\label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}

The overhead ratio captures the expansion factor:

\begin{equation}
R_{\text{overhead}} = \frac{N_{\text{DNA}}}{B_{\text{original}} \times 8 / 2}
\label{eq:overhead_ratio}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Biological Realism Metrics}

Shannon entropy of the encoded DNA is computed as:

\begin{equation}
H = -\sum_{b \in \{A,T,G,C\}} p_b \log_2 p_b
\label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}

\noindent with maximum $H_{\max} = 2.0$ for four equiprobable bases. GC-content is defined as:

\begin{equation}
GC = \frac{n_G + n_C}{N}
\label{eq:gc}
\end{equation}

The composite realism score combines three sub-scores with empirically chosen weights:

\begin{equation}
R = 0.4 \times S_{GC} + 0.35 \times S_H + 0.25 \times S_B
\label{eq:realism}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $S_{GC}$ is the GC-content score (1.0 if $GC \in [0.35, 0.65]$, linearly decaying outside), $S_H = \min(1.0, H/2.0)$ is the entropy score, and $S_B = \max(0, 1 - 4 \times \overline{|p_b - 0.25|})$ is the base balance score.

Additional biological indicators include dinucleotide frequency analysis and the CpG ratio (observed CG dinucleotide frequency divided by expected frequency from marginal base probabilities), which is characteristically suppressed ($\sim$0.25$\times$ expected) in mammalian genomes.

\subsection{Naive vs. Constrained Comparison}

The evaluation framework produces a side-by-side comparison between naive 2-bit encoding and the constrained rotation cipher, measuring realism score, maximum homopolymer run, GC window compliance, restriction site presence, and overall constraint compliance. The improvement is quantified as:

\begin{align}
\Delta R &= R_{\text{constrained}} - R_{\text{naive}} \label{eq:delta_r} \\
\Delta h &= h_{\text{naive}} - h_{\text{constrained}} \label{eq:delta_h} \\
\Delta m &= m_{\text{naive}} - m_{\text{constrained}} \label{eq:delta_m}
\end{align}

\noindent where $h$ denotes maximum homopolymer run length and $m$ denotes the number of forbidden motifs present.

The system supports any file type as input. Uploaded files are automatically deleted after processing for privacy. The DNA output, AES key, and recovered files are cleaned before each new operation.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Strengths}

BioVerse provides a complete, deployable pipeline from arbitrary files to biologically viable DNA sequences with integrated security, error correction, constraint enforcement, and tamper detection. The evaluation framework produces quantitative metrics suitable for comparative studies, and the web interface makes the system accessible to non-expert users.

The rotation cipher approach has the advantage of simplicity and determinism: given the same input and rotation flags, the encoding is exactly reversible with zero information loss. The sideband metadata overhead ($N/4$ bytes for $N$ bases) is modest compared to the DNA payload.

\subsection{Limitations}

\subsubsection{Storage Overhead}
AES encryption expands data by one block (16 bytes), and Reed-Solomon adds 32 parity bytes per block, yielding effective density of approximately 0.4--0.5 bits/base for small files versus the theoretical 2.0 and the 1.98 achieved by DNA Fountain \cite{b4}. This is a direct tradeoff for security and error resilience that existing high-density systems do not provide.

\subsubsection{Hash Embedding Granularity}
The 4-to-1 hex-to-base mapping (Equation~\eqref{eq:hex_mapping}) reduces hash entropy from 4 bits per hex character to 2 bits per DNA base. This introduces a non-negligible collision probability in the embedded hash comparison. A more information-theoretically efficient embedding scheme (e.g., 2-bit-per-base direct binary encoding of the hash) could improve tamper detection precision.

\subsubsection{Greedy Constraint Resolution}
The rotation cipher resolves constraint violations locally using a per-position greedy search. A global optimization approach, such as constrained coding theory \cite{b5} or Viterbi decoding over a constrained trellis, could reduce the rotation rate and potentially improve effective encoding density.

\subsubsection{AI Component Scope}
The LSTM fingerprint generator provides biological camouflage but does not directly improve encoding density or error correction. The camouflage effect is proportional to the fingerprint-to-payload ratio, which decreases for larger files.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This paper presented BioVerse, an AI-assisted DNA data encoding system with three contributions: biologically-constrained encoding using a rotation cipher, dual-purpose integrity watermarking via embedded SHA-256 hashes in AI-generated fingerprints, and a quantitative evaluation framework for standardized benchmarking. Experimental results confirm lossless data recovery, full biological constraint compliance, and functional tamper detection.

Future work includes: (1) replacing the greedy rotation with a capacity-approaching constrained code for improved density; (2) implementing HMAC-based integrity embedding for stronger tamper resistance with lower collision probability; (3) error simulation experiments with varying mutation, insertion, and deletion rates to characterize resilience boundaries; (4) compression pre-processing (e.g., LZ4, zstd) to improve effective encoding density before encryption; and (5) integration with DNA synthesis order APIs for end-to-end wet-lab validation.
